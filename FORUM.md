## IC upgrades

_WBS TECHNICAL SUPPORT · Aug 15, 2010|43_

> It's been a while since I've been inside the W/B toys...
>
> When i was fixing up a bunch of modules, I dropped in TL071's in place of the single channel opamps that I suspected to be faulty... the pins are the same, minus 1,5 and 8... sometimes I would bend those pins up from the new IC, so that they didn't connect in the sockets.
> the originals used them for frequency compensation, while the 071's used them to set the Off-Set...
> (This is all from memory, so double check the pin-out if you try it!)
>
> If the pin-out of a new op-amp lines up... it will work!
>
> Also, while cleaning up a console filled with M480c's, one of the channels had a blown Pre-Amp... one of the transistors went dead.
> I didn't have a direct replacement, so I put in a Low-Noise BC549... I built my Neve clones out of them...
>
> The channel worked... and at max gain, with the fader all the way up... it was more than 4 times quieter than the stock channel strips!
> I'm talking about very little Hiss and Pops and Crackling!
>
> I couldn't leave one channel that clean... so I ended up replacing every preamp transistor in every other channel!
>
> Did it affect the tone? I didn't really test... I told the owner what I did, and to let me know what he thought...
> The only word back I got was: 'These pre's sound f-ing sick!'
>
> I remember a post somewhere on Geoff Tanner's forum... I think he put up a story, where the owner of a Neve console asked him if he did a phase-check on the entire console...

## M480 Pad

_WBS TECHNICAL SUPPORT · Dec 21, 2012|45_

> I modified the M480C shematic to show what's actually in an M480 before the input transformer. Dashed lines indicate full range of switch (poles are not in order).

— **KidComa**, Oct 25, 2013|46

> The asymmetry can be caused by a differential through/across the op-amps yes. So lets visit the in-module regulator for a balanced voltage distribution, then lets visit the voltages across the pre-amp section (meaning each transistor make certain they are equal in consumption of power). So start there. What I have done is measured each regulator section, made detailed notes, and then selected a MASTER module. Tweaked each subsequent module to be equal to that master module. Then on the pre-section I did the same thing. Measured each transistor, determined if I had something with which I could work, and then matched components form there. Also this was done on the EQ section. This brings you damn close to what Jimmy, Wai-Man, and Wai-Keung used to do when they felt the need of truly matching the amplifyers to each other. A huge job - Jimmy had a jig to match the transistors and this made life easy. I was given that task many, many times as a kid as it kept me in a corner and quiet - not bothering anyone basically! I am currently doing this to my M460's and my little T1202A (M480C - THANK YOU APK AUDIO!!!!).
>
> So when you are on ebay or some poop like that and someone says "Matched pair of preamps" you KNOW they are BS'ing you for the only way to match them is the above procedure. It takes a LOT of manual labour, and is expensive. The components are virtually all replaced during the matching process, thus are no longer vintage, and with the labour involved you can expect to pay an additional $500 and up per module for this process.
>
> OK off topic again Warren ... so do not worry about the asymmetry - it can be fixed but it is largely un-important.
>

## No stereo signal out of the L1202

_WBS TECHNICAL SUPPORT · Jun 26, 2013|46_

> Well a L1202 (T1202??)is really a dual mono board - for sterio you will have to visit the world's first true sterio consoles the ST series that we made/invented. 
>
> But that is semantics. You should be sending signal out of M1 and M2 if all the wiring is complete, if the faders are hooked in place, if the summing modules are not damaged, the assignment module(s - depending upon the version) is assigning the audio in the correct manner to your monitoring out-put, and lastly you must assign the input channel to either M1 or M2 to thus route the signal to an output. If you have the M480C module the pan control should be in the centre position, and if you are sending signal in to the little console on input A make certain that you have activated the appropriate channel, and have selected input A - as each module M480, M480A, M480B, and M480C all have 2 inputs per module.
>
> Also Tony has scanned and posted many manuals here on the WBSPS, and I believe there is a T1202 manual there and a couple of L series as well, to help you with signal routing, etc...
>
> Regards,
